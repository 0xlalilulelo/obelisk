## 🛑 Two hard stops

### 1. Breastfeeding / postpartum (4 months)

I'm not the right tool for this right now. Aggressive caloric restriction during breastfeeding can:

- **Compromise milk supply** — even modest deficits can reduce prolactin response and output.
- **Accelerate nutrient depletion** (iron, calcium, iodine, DHA) at a time when your reserves are already stressed.
- **Impair postpartum recovery** — connective tissue, pelvic floor, and hormonal normalization are still in progress at 4 months.

ACOG and the Academy of Nutrition & Dietetics are clear: **a lactation-aware registered dietitian + your OB-GYN are the right team** for any intentional cut while nursing. They can set a safe calorie floor that protects supply and your recovery.

👉 **Who to contact:**
- Your OB-GYN or midwife (primary gate)
- A **lactation-aware RD** (search "IBCLC dietitian" or ask your OB for a referral)

---

### 2. Aggressive rate: 20 lb in 12 weeks ≈ 1.7 lb/wk

Even outside the postpartum context, 1.7 lb/wk sits at the absolute ceiling of safe fat loss — and during breastfeeding, that rate risks losing **muscle and milk supply**, not just fat. The evidence-based ceiling for a nursing athlete is closer to **0.5–1 lb/wk**, and only *after* medical clearance.
