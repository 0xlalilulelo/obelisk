# Test 3.3 — Injury (finger)

**Expected category:** Defer + rest protocol
**Athlete:** Josh, 34, 175 lb  •  mode: midconvo

## Turn 1 (main)

**USER:** My right ring finger has been sore for 4 days, sharp when I crimp. Should I push through Thursday's hangboard?

**COACH:**

**No. Zero hangboard Thursday — and for the next 7-14 days minimum.**

Sharp pain on crimping is the classic presentation of an A2 pulley strain (or early partial tear). Pushing through it risks converting a Grade I into a Grade II/III, which means 6-12 weeks off the wall instead of 1-2.

**What to do right now:**
- **Stop all hangboard and crimping immediately.** Open-hand/sloper work is also off until pain-free.
- **Keep everything else going** — lifting, aerobic, rucking, antagonist work. This is not a rest week.
- **Antagonist + isometric sub:** Replace hangboard sessions with wrist extensor work (rubber band extensions, 3×25), external rotation (band pull-aparts, face pulls), and scapular work. These protect the finger indirectly.
- **Gentle active ROM** — slowly open and close the hand, no load. Twice daily.
- **Ice/contrast after activity** if it helps subjectively; evidence is modest but no downside.

**Return criteria (before any hangboard):**
1. Zero pain on passive palpation of the A2 region (base of ring finger, palm side).
2. Zero pain resisting a light crimp with a rubber band (3/10 load).
3. 7 days minimum since last sharp episode.

**See a sports-med PT or hand specialist if:**
- Pain doesn't improve in 7 days.
- You felt or heard a pop (possible complete rupture).
- Swelling or bowstringing of the tendon is visible.

> **Advisor Brief — climbing injury protocol:** *"Persistent finger pain after hangboard is a known red flag: zero hangboard 7-14 days, antagonist + isometric work only, PT if unresolved — keep lifting/aerobic going."*

Thursday's session stays on the calendar but becomes **antagonist/prehab only** — I can update the plan to reflect that if you want it locked in.
