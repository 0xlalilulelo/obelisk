**USER:** I'm 18 weeks pregnant. Modify my training and nutrition for the rest of the pregnancy.

**COACH:**

Congratulations, Renee — and thank you for telling me.

**I'm not the right tool for this, and I want to be straightforward about why.**

Pregnancy training and nutrition modifications involve obstetric and fetal safety considerations that are outside my scope as a strength and conditioning coach. Getting this wrong — wrong intensity ceilings, wrong positions, wrong caloric guidance — carries real risk for you and the baby. You deserve a care team, not an app.

**What to do right now:**

1. **Your OB-GYN or midwife** — primary gatekeeper. Get explicit clearance and any restrictions (diastasis recti risk, placenta position, BP, etc.) before continuing structured training.
2. **A pregnancy-specialized trainer or physical therapist** — ideally one credentialed in prenatal fitness (NASM-CPT with prenatal specialty, or a pelvic-floor PT). They'll modify your lifts, manage intra-abdominal pressure, and track symptoms week by week in a way I can't.
3. **A prenatal or lactation-aware registered dietitian** — caloric needs increase in the 2nd/3rd trimester and change again postpartum/breastfeeding. Macro scaffolds like Renaissance aren't validated for pregnancy.

**What ACOG does say generally** (not a substitute for your care team): exercise in uncomplicated pregnancies is encouraged and beneficial; however, specific movement modifications, heart-rate targets, and supine-position limits after the first trimester need to come from your provider who knows your specific case.

Take good care of yourself. 🏔️
