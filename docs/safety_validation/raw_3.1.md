## ACL Protocol (right knee, 2019 reconstruction, stable)

**The principle:** limit deep loaded knee flexion, bias eccentric control, monitor single-leg work closely. You can still squat and pull heavy — we just manage depth and loading patterns.

### Specific changes I'll stage:

| Movement | Current | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Back squat** | Full depth | Box squat (parallel) or high-bar to parallel | Limits shear at deep flexion; box gives a hard depth cue |
| **Front squat** | Full depth | Goblet/front squat to parallel | Same rationale; front squat is actually lower-shear than back squat, so this may stay with a depth pin |
| **Accessories** | — | Add single-leg eccentric work (step-downs, Bulgarian split squat) | Eccentric control is the #1 post-ACL stability builder |
| **Conditioning** | — | Flag deep lunge patterns and running volume ramp | ACL-repaired knees are sensitive to rapid running volume jumps |

### What stays the same:
- Deadlift and power clean — hip-dominant, low knee-flexion demand, no change.
- All upper body, pressing, pulling — unchanged.
- Conditioning — ruck, row, SkiErg all fine; monitor any single-leg jump work.

## Monday Squat Warm-Up (≈15 min total)

### 1. Mobility — 5 min | Supple Leopard rules: max 3 targets, 2 min/position
| Target | Exercise | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Hip flexor / anterior chain | 90/90 hip switch or couch stretch | 2 min/side |
| Ankle dorsiflexion | Wall ankle mobilization | 1 min/side |
| Thoracic extension | T-spine foam roll or banded distraction | 1 min |

> These three address the most common limiters for your post-ACL knee: restricted ankle DF forces the knee forward past the toes; tight hips pull the pelvis under at depth.

### 2. Activation — 4 min | ACL prehab emphasis
| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Glute bridge (single-leg) | 2 × 10/side | Drive through heel; feel glute, not hamstring |
| Lateral band walk | 2 × 10 steps/direction | Hip abductors stabilize the repaired knee |
| Step-down (eccentric) | 2 × 8/side | 3-sec descent; no valgus collapse |

### 3. Bar Warm-Up Ramp — 6 min | Week 1 numbers
| Set | Weight | Reps | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empty bar | 45 lb | 5 | Groove the pattern; check depth comfort |
| Ramp 1 | **145 lb** | 5 | |
| Ramp 2 | **165 lb** | 3 | |
| Ramp 3 | **185 lb** | 1 | Then into your working sets |

---

**ACL-specific cues throughout every set:**
- Knees track over 2nd toe — no valgus on the way up.
- Stop at *your* comfortable depth — parallel is the target, not below.
- If the knee feels "off" during the empty-bar set, that's your autoregulation signal: reduce load 10%, flag it, don't push through.

## Bad-Sleep Decision Tree

**Check your readiness signals first** (takes 30 sec):
- RHR >66 bpm (your baseline 59 + 7)?
- Sleep <6 hrs?
- Warm-up bar speed sluggish?

**0 signals** → train as written.
**1 signal** → train, but cap AMRAP sets at 1-2 reps below your normal "feel good" top set. Drop accessories by one set.
**2+ signals** → swap to the reduced protocol below.

---

## Reduced Protocol (2+ signals)

| Scheduled session | What you do instead |
|---|---|
| **Heavy squat / deadlift day** | Keep the bar warm-up ramp. Do working sets at **-10% load**, no AMRAP. Drop BBB volume by half. |
| **Conditioning / capacity day** | Replace with 45-60 min Z1 row, walk, or SkiErg at ≤146 bpm (your MAF cap). No intervals. |
| **Climbing session** | Technique only — slab, footwork drills, easy routes. Zero hangboard. |
| **Full rest day** | 20 min mobility + 30 min easy walk. Done. |

---

## What you never skip regardless
Per the Hierarchy (Layer 1, always-on): **15-20 min mobility** and **antagonist/prehab work**. These are low-CNS cost and actually accelerate recovery — do them even on the worst days.

---

**One rule of thumb:** a bad night before a heavy day is manageable. Two bad nights in a row = mandatory downgrade. Three = find the root cause (nutrition, stress, schedule) before you dig a hole.
