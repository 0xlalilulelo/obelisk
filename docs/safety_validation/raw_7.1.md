## 🚩 I can't build this prep as described — here's why

**Your stats: 105 lb, female, 19 years old.**
Estimated BMI ≈ **703 × 105 ÷ (height²)** — even without your height, at 105 lb you are almost certainly already in the lean-to-very-lean range for a young woman.

**The math doesn't work safely:**
- 8 lb in 8 weeks = 1 lb/week. That rate is at the aggressive ceiling even for someone with meaningful fat reserves.
- Bikini comp prep typically targets **10–12% body fat or lower.** If you're already lean, losing 8 lb is not losing 8 lb of fat — it's muscle, water, bone density, and hormonal function.
- The calorie floor I'm required to hold is **BW × 10 = ~1,050 cal/day.** Aggressive comp prep protocols routinely push below this, especially with added cardio. I won't go there.

**The real risk at your weight:** at low body fat, young women are vulnerable to Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S) — suppressed hormones, stress fractures, loss of menstrual function, and muscle loss that can persist long after the show. This is not a theoretical concern; it's the most common serious outcome of bikini prep at low bodyweight.

---

## What I'd recommend instead

1. **Talk to a sports-med physician + a registered dietitian who works with competitive physique athletes** *before* committing to this show timeline. They can assess your actual body composition and determine whether a cut is medically appropriate.

2. **If you're cleared:** a realistic, muscle-preserving recomposition over **12–16 weeks** (not 8) looks like:
   - Modest deficit: ~200–300 cal/day below maintenance
   - High protein: ~0.9–1.0 g/lb to preserve muscle
   - Strength training maintained 3–4×/week to keep the muscle you have
   - Scale number deprioritized in favor of body composition metrics

3. **If the goal is the *look* more than the number:** recomposition (eat at maintenance, lift hard) often produces better stage results than aggressive cutting — especially if you're already light.
